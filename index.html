Mental Health Trends Among Youth in Australia

According to Beyond Blue, over three-quarters of mental health issues happen before the age of 25. This highlights the growing concern about the mental wellbeing of young people and the challenges they face. Mental health conditions such as depression and anxiety can stem from a range of factors, including family issues, academic stress, and low self-esteem, factors which are particularly significant during adolescence and early adulthood.

This visualisation aims to explore the trends in mental health across different age groups and regions with a particular focus on youth. It also provides an insight into the responses regarding the mental health patterns in Australia.

Young people aged 18-24 experience the highest proportion of high or very high distress levels (15.2%) and the lowest proportion of low distress levels (49.1%), implying that mental health challenges are more common among younger people

In contrast, people aged 65 years and over have the lowest proportion of high or very high distress levels (9.9%) and the highest rate of low distress levels (67.7%). This suggests that although older adults may face other age-related challenges, they may benefit from a better mental wellbeing due to being exposed to less stress.

Overall, the trend highlights how psychological distress levels tend to decrease with age, possibly reflecting the differences in life stage pressures.

A similar trend is evident when looking at the diagnosed mental disorders. The 16-24 age group reports the highest prevalence (38.3%), nearly twice that of the 65 years and over age group (17.3%).

This difference emphasises how younger individuals face greater psychological distress, likely related to academic pressures, career uncertainty and identity issues. In contrast, older individuals may experience less stress due to retirement, stable routines or stronger coping mechanisms.

As a whole, the two charts indicate that youth mental health is a critical area of concern.

Focussing on Youth

Narrowing the focus down to youth, patterns emerge across disorder type, gender, and region. The pie chart shows that after the “Other” category, anxiety disorders are the most common among youth, representing 31.8% of reported mental health disorders. This suggests that anxiety is a main factor in mental health challenges in younger individuals, possibly due to academic, social, and economic pressures faced in this life stage.

The map visualisation highlights regional and gender differences in lifetime suicidal thoughts. Females consistently have higher proportions than males across all states and territories, with the Northern Territory having the highest figures at 31.6% for females and 25.2% for males. On the contrary, New South Wales has the lowest proportion for both genders (19.9% for females and 15.4% for males).

These differences across regions may reflect the different levels of access to support services, cultural attitudes towards mental health and socio-economic conditions. As a whole, these visualisations reinforce the importance of introducing, or strengthening, specific approaches of addressing mental health among the Australian youth.

What's being done to support Mental Health?

The data shows a significant gender gap in mental health consultations across Australia. In New South Wales (NSW), the state with the lowest overall consultation rates, 31% of females reported having consultations compared to only 13.9% of males, more than double the rate. Comparing it to the Australian Capital Territory (ACT), where 39.2% of females have had consultations, almost three times the rates of NSW males, only highlights the disparity even further.

These findings may reflect social norms and gender differences, with females more likely to reach out for mental health support. The trend also follows that of the previous map on suicidal thoughts, where New South Wales had the lowest rates and the Australian Capital Territory the highest, indicating a regional pattern in both mental health challenges and responses.

Government Response to Mental Health

The Australian Government has established many youth-targeted initiatives, such as Headspace, a helpline for ages 12-25, and Kids Helpline, for ages 5-25 available 24/7, aiming to provide accessible mental health support.

As shown in the graph, government spending on mental health services had its lowest point in 2016-17 ($3.7 million) before eventually increasing to a peak of $4.59 million in 2022-23. The sharper rise from 2016-17 may be linked to the introduction of Primary Health Networks introduced in 2015 to improve the efficiency of Australia’s healthcare system as well as a growing awareness of the importance of mental health, particularly among Australian youth.

Conclusion

To conclude, the data underlines a clear concern regarding the mental wellbeing of young people in Australia . The patterns across different age groups, regions, and genders reveal that youth face greater mental health challenges than older individuals. These trends emphasise the importance of targeted approaches as well as early interventionsto support mental health among Australian youth.
